### §B.3.3 The "Pi Dimensionality" Question

**What "pi dimensionality" means in the QNFO context:**

The memory about π in non-Archimedean completions is the key insight: *"When porting a geometric constant like π to non-Archimedean completions, define it via the same ratio (circumference/diameter) using local field geometry and boundary Haar measure, not by analytically extending the real decimal expansion."*

This is directly relevant to ODR's Ostrowski rationale because:
- π is the paradigmatic dimensionless number — the ratio of circumference to diameter
- In the Archimedean completion (ℝ), π = 3.14159... as a convergent infinite series
- In a p-adic completion (ℚ_p), the same ratio C/d may NOT converge to the same number because the p-adic metric assigns different sizes to the terms of the series
- **π is not well-defined as a real number in a p-adic completion** — its decimal expansion has no p-adic meaning
- BUT π AS A RATIO (C/d) is definable in any geometry, including p-adic geometries

**Relevance to ODR:** The ODR paper's Ostrowski rationale (§2.1) states that pure numbers are "equally well-defined at every place." The π dimensionality question challenges this: is π equally well-defined at every place? The answer: π AS A GEOMETRIC RATIO is definable at every place; π AS THE REAL NUMBER 3.14159... is defined only at the Archimedean place. This is the exact same reasoning ODR applies to physical constants like ℏ and c — the ratio form is place-democratic; the decimal expansion is Archimedean.
